# Business Problem — Supplier Carbon Risk Observatory

## The Question
Which suppliers and procurement categories are creating the greatest carbon
risk, and where can the company reduce emissions without unnecessarily
increasing procurement cost or disrupting supply?

## Why This Matters
Most companies report only a fraction of their real emissions if they ignore
their supply chain. Supply chain emissions (Scope 3) typically represent
70-90% of a company's total carbon footprint, yet procurement and
sustainability teams often work with disconnected data: procurement tracks
cost and delivery, sustainability tracks emissions, and the two are rarely
analyzed together.

## Who Uses This
- Procurement Manager
- Sustainability / ESG Manager
- Supply Chain Director

## Decisions This Dashboard Supports
1. Identify which suppliers and categories drive the most emissions.
2. Distinguish "high-spend" suppliers from "high-carbon-risk" suppliers —
   they are not always the same.
3. Evaluate a decarbonization scenario: what happens to emissions and cost
   if a portion of spend shifts away from high-carbon suppliers.
4. Prioritize supplier engagement or replacement based on data, not guesswork.

## Scope and Approach
This project estimates emissions using a spend/activity-based and
distance-based method, combining public procurement/transaction data with
official government emission factors and country-distance data. This
mirrors how many real organizations estimate Scope 3 Category 1 (Purchased
Goods & Services) emissions in practice, since direct supplier-reported
emissions data is rarely available.
